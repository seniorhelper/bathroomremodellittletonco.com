The short answer

In Littleton, Colorado, a midrange full bathroom remodel runs $10,000–$18,000 and a full custom tile bathroom runs $15,000–$25,000+. If you're only doing the shower: a one-day Crestline install starts at $6,900, a tub-to-shower conversion runs $8,500–$14,500, and a custom tile shower runs $12,000–$20,000+. Supply-only shower pans start under $2,000 if you're installing yourself, and budget-friendly shower remodels can finish under $10,000 fully installed.

Plenty of Colorado contractors quote $30,000 or more for a single custom shower. Sometimes that's justified. Often it's franchise fees and television advertising. The rest of this page is how to tell which one you're looking at.

Does size matter?

Less than you'd think for showers. A lot for full remodels.

A shower is a shower. Whether it sits in a 35-square-foot hall bath downtown or a 120-square-foot Highlands Ranch master, the wall system, the pan, the valve, and the labor to install them are broadly similar. Shower-only projects barely scale with room size.

Full remodels are different, because flooring, lighting, vanity size, and tile quantity all grow with the footprint. Rough scaling against a small bathroom:

Up or down

The eight things that actually move your price.

Two identical-looking bathrooms two blocks apart in Littleton can quote thousands apart, and it's almost never about the quality of the work. It's these.

01

Does the drain move?

Keeping the existing drain location is the single biggest cost saver in any bathroom project. Moving it means opening the floor and, in most jurisdictions, a permit.

Adds $1,500–$4,000
02

Subfloor condition

What's underneath a forty-year-old pan is the one thing nobody can quote sight unseen. Sound subfloor, no change. Rot or soft spots, and it gets addressed properly before anything new goes down.

Varies — inspected first
03

Panel system or custom tile

Crestline is a one-day install with no grout and a 30-year manufacturer warranty. Tile is days of skilled labor plus waterproofing and cure time. Both are excellent. They cost differently.

Panel saves 30–45%
04

Doors and glass

A framed slider versus frameless heavy glass can differ by thousands. This is the biggest swing most quotes hide inside a lump sum. We price it as its own line so you can see it and decide.

$400 – $4,000+
05

Valve and plumbing work

Swapping a trim kit is inexpensive. Replacing the valve body, or upgrading to a thermostatic anti-scald valve, means opening a wall. Worth doing in any home with kids or older adults.

Adds $600–$2,500
06

Curbless entry

Zero-threshold requires either a recessed subfloor or a ramped low-profile pan, plus a linear drain. Genuinely worth it if you plan to stay in the home, but it is structural work and it isn't free.

Adds $2,000–$5,000
07

Layout changes

Keeping the existing footprint keeps the project predictable. Enlarging the shower into a closet or moving the vanity wall is a different project with a different number.

Keeping layout saves most

Permit costs

Small line, large consequences.

Permit fees are set by whichever jurisdiction covers your parcel — City of Littleton, Arapahoe County, Jefferson County, or Douglas County — and each publishes its own schedule, which changes periodically. In practice it's a small fraction of a bathroom budget. A like-for-like fixture swap usually needs no permit. Moving a drain, relocating a valve, adding a circuit or exhaust fan, or enlarging the shower does. We pull it and pass the fee through at cost. The reason this matters isn't the fee — it's that unpermitted bathroom work is one of the top items that renegotiates or kills a Denver-metro real estate deal at inspection.

Return at resale

What you get back when you sell.

The Zonda Cost vs. Value Report consistently places midrange bathroom remodels in the 74–80% national ROI range, near the top of where that category has sat in recent years. In south Denver specifically, bathrooms and kitchens are the two rooms buyers evaluate first. Dated tile, a cracked tub, or visibly unpermitted work tends to cost more in reduced offers than the remodel would have cost outright. Universal-design elements — curbless entry, grab bars, comfort-height fixtures — widen the buyer pool rather than narrowing it.

Timing & financing

When to book, and how to pay for it.

Late winter through early spring is the best window in the Denver metro: more crew availability, fewer backorders, and if you're selling into the summer market you finish with weeks to spare. Bathroom work is interior, so the slower season is an advantage rather than a constraint. On financing, we work with several home improvement lenders offering low-monthly-payment plans, deferred-interest promotions, and long-term fixed-rate options. Rates depend on the lender, amount, term, and credit profile, so we present real terms in writing at the consultation rather than publishing a payment table that would be wrong by the time you read it.

Before you sign anything

How to compare three quotes fairly.

Print this. Take it to all three appointments. Quotes that differ by thousands almost always differ in scope, not in value — and this is how you find out which.

  1. Fixed or "starting at"? Get the words "fixed price" in writing. "Starting at" exists because change orders are where a lot of remodelers make their real margin.
  2. Workmanship warranty — what, and how long? Most Colorado remodelers offer one year on labor. Ours is five. Read the actual document, not the brochure.
  3. Who physically does the work? An employed crew you've met, or day-labor subs you'll meet on day one. There's a real quality-control difference.
  4. Are permits included and pulled? If the answer is a shrug, that's your resale disclosure and your homeowner's insurance they're shrugging at.
  5. What's the waterproofing method? Correct answers: Schluter Kerdi membrane, RedGard, or a pre-manufactured sloped pan system. A blank stare is an answer too.
  6. Are doors and glass a separate line? If they're buried in a lump sum, you can't tell whether you're being quoted a framed slider or frameless heavy glass.
  7. What happens if it runs long? A fixed calendar, or "we should be done by whenever." Ask before, not during.
