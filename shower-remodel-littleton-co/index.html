The nine things that move your number.

1. Does the drain move? Keeping the existing drain location is the single biggest cost saver in any shower project. Moving it means opening the floor.

2. Subfloor condition. What's under a forty-year-old pan is the one thing nobody can quote sight unseen. Sound subfloor, no change. Rot, and it gets addressed properly.

3. Panel or tile. Crestline is a one-day install with no grout. Tile is days of labor plus cure time. Both are excellent, they just cost differently.

4. Doors and glass. A framed slider and a frameless heavy-glass panel can differ by thousands. This is the swing most quotes hide inside a lump sum.

5. Valve work. Swapping a trim kit is cheap. Relocating a valve or upgrading to thermostatic anti-scald means opening a wall.

6. Curbless or curbed. Zero-threshold entry requires structural work at the floor. Worth every dollar if you're planning to stay in the home, but it is not free.

7. Size and configuration. Standard alcove, corner, neo-angle, or enlarging into adjacent space. The last one is a different project entirely.

8. Permits. Required when plumbing or electrical moves. Cheap compared to what unpermitted work costs you at resale.

9. Who installs it. Turnkey through us, or supply-only through Showers4Less with your own contractor. Both are real options here.

Where Littleton showers get complicated.

Downtown Littleton's 1890s–1940s bungalows and cottages have small bathrooms, plaster walls, cast-iron drains, and framing that measures whatever it measured a century ago. Panel systems built to standard dimensions often don't fit these rooms cleanly, which is why custom tile tends to win downtown. We build around the quirks rather than pretending they aren't there.

The 1960s–1980s ranches and split-levels through Columbine, Ken Caryl, and south Littleton are the opposite: predictable rough-in, standard alcove tub footprints, and a hall bath that has been waiting fifteen years for someone to do something about it. These are the fastest, cleanest tub-to-shower conversions we do and they price accordingly.

Highlands Ranch and Roxborough new-builds from the 2000s onward usually have a garden tub nobody has filled since the walkthrough plus a builder-grade shower next to it. Converting that tub into a large walk-in with a bench is one of the highest-satisfaction projects in the whole catalog.

Whatever the housing stock, the honest sequence is the same: we look at the room, we tell you which of the six project types you're actually in, and you get a fixed written number before anything is committed.
