Hiring a bathroom contractor in Littleton? Check these first.

The license, insurance and lead-safe checks that take about ten minutes.

The short answer

Inside City of Littleton limits, a contractor needs a City of Littleton license to pull a building permit, with $1,000,000 per occurrence and $2,000,000 aggregate in general liability insurance on file. The plumber and electrician on your job need Colorado state licenses. If the home was built before 1978, the firm should be EPA lead-safe certified.

Those four checks sort out most of the risk before you ever compare prices. They take about ten minutes with a phone and a laptop, and a good contractor will hand you the numbers before you ask. The rest of this page walks through each one, then covers what a fair written quote should contain and the questions that tell you how the job will actually run.

Step one

Which licenses does a Littleton bathroom job need?

Usually two layers: the city license for the company, and state licenses for the trades.

The City of Littleton says plainly that "a license or registration certificate is required to obtain building permits to do work within the city limits of Littleton," and the license holder has to keep it on hand while working. The city issues several contractor classes (A, B, C, plus D-5 for roofing and D-11 for HVAC and mechanical), and new applicants may have to pass an International Code Council exam. Licenses expire every December 31, so a license that was valid last year has to have been renewed.

Plumbing and electrical work sits on top of that. Littleton requires proof of an active State of Colorado master or journeyman license, and a state contractor license, that match the record kept by the Colorado Department of Regulatory Agencies (DORA). Colorado law also says a business cannot operate as a plumbing contractor until it is registered with the state plumbing board, and that a master plumber must supervise all of the contractor's plumbing work.

One wrinkle is specific to this area. Many homes with a Littleton mailing address are not inside the city at all. Highlands Ranch, Ken Caryl and much of Columbine fall under Douglas, Jefferson or Arapahoe County, which run their own permits. If that is you, ask the county building division what it requires of contractors. Our Littleton permit guide shows how to tell which office covers your address.

Step two

How do you check a contractor's insurance?

Ask for a certificate of insurance before you sign, and read three things on it: the policy dates, the general liability limits, and whether workers' compensation is listed. The City of Littleton requires every licensed contractor to carry general liability of $1,000,000 per occurrence and $2,000,000 aggregate, with the city named as certificate holder, and workers' compensation for any company with employees. A company with no employees can file a waiver instead.

That waiver matters to you. If a crew member is hurt in your bathroom and the company carries no workers' compensation, you want to know that before work starts, not after. It is a fair question to ask who exactly will be in your home, whether they are employees or subcontractors, and whose insurance covers each of them.

Step three

Does your Littleton home need a lead-safe contractor?

If the house was built before 1978, quite possibly. Many of Littleton's ranches, split-levels and older condos were built before then, and lead-based paint was banned for homes only in 1978. Under the EPA's Renovation, Repair and Painting rule, renovators working in those homes must be certified, and they must give you the EPA's "Renovate Right" pamphlet before work starts. The rule kicks in for interior projects that disturb more than six square feet of painted surface in a room, which a tub tear-out or a wall opened for a new valve can easily do.

You can look up a certified firm at epa.gov/getleadsafe or by calling the National Lead Information Center at 1-800-424-LEAD (5323). If a contractor working in a 1960s bathroom has never heard of the pamphlet, keep looking.

Step four

What should a written bathroom quote include?

Every line you can see is a line you can compare.

A one-line quote ("shower remodel, complete") tells you nothing you can check against another bid. Ask for these items on separate lines: removal and disposal of the old tub or shower; the new fixture by brand and model; the pan or base; the wall system or tile; the valve and any plumbing changes; electrical work; doors or glass; grab bars and blocking; permit and inspection; and cleanup. Ask which items are fixed and which depend on what is found once the old tub is out, such as a soft subfloor.

Then ask the questions that tell you how the job will run:

  1. Who pulls the permit? The licensed contractor should, and you should get the permit number.
  2. Who will be in my home? Names or at least roles: employees, subcontractors, and who supervises the plumbing.
  3. When is the deposit due, and what does it buy? Get the payment schedule in writing, tied to milestones.
  4. How long will the bathroom be out of service? Many tub and shower swaps take one day; full remodels take longer.
  5. What warranty covers the product, and what covers the labor? Read the written terms for both.

Littleton specifics

What do Littleton homes usually need from a contractor?

Many Littleton bathrooms are still original to a ranch, split-level or townhome built between the 1960s and 1980s: a 60-inch tub alcove, a single hall bath upstairs, and often a second bath in a finished basement. That shapes the contractor you need. A tub-to-shower conversion in that kind of house touches the drain, the valve and the wall, so it is plumbing work under permit, not a cosmetic job. If the person using the bathroom is getting older, ask whether the contractor can add blocking for grab bars while the wall is open, and whether a low-threshold or roll-in shower will fit.
