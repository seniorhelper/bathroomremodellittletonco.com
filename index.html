The Littleton bathroom remodel buyer's guide

What the guys with the TV commercials hope you never ask.

Eighteen years of Colorado bathroom remodels have taught us which decisions actually matter and which ones the industry gets away with hand-waving through. Here's the honest walkthrough — no jargon dressed up as expertise, no scare tactics.

Tile vs. Crestline: which one wins for your shower?

This is the first real fork in the road for anyone remodeling a bathroom in Littleton, and the honest answer is that both are excellent — for different reasons. Custom tile gives you total design freedom. You can do a floor-to-ceiling herringbone Calacatta feature wall, a niche in exactly the spot you want, a curbless zero-threshold entry with a linear drain, or a matte black hex mosaic that ties into the vanity. Every dimension is bespoke. Tile is the answer when you want the shower to be the design statement of the whole bathroom and you have the budget and timeline for it. It also stays beautiful for decades if the waterproofing is done correctly — the tile itself is basically forever.

Crestline goes the other direction: it wins on speed, cleanliness, and ongoing maintenance. A Crestline shower installs in a single day. There is no grout to scrub, ever. The panel is 3/8-inch high-pressure laminate on a marine-grade backer with a factory vapor barrier, and it comes in four convincing stone patterns — grey slate, dark grey marble, beige limestone, and white marble. The manufacturer warranty is 30 years. For a rental property, a second bathroom, a shower you use every day and never want to think about again, or a house you're prepping for market in the next six months, Crestline is often the smarter money.

Where do most Littleton clients land? Roughly 60% Crestline, 40% custom tile — with tile being more common in downtown historic homes and master baths, Crestline being the go-to for guest baths, tub-to-shower conversions, and any project where the calendar is tight.

The seven questions every Littleton homeowner should ask before signing a contract.

1. Is the quote fixed or "starting at"? A written fixed-price quote is not negotiable. "Starting at" pricing exists because change orders are how a lot of remodelers make their real margin.

2. Who is physically doing the work? An employed crew you've met or day-labor subs you'll meet on day one? There's a real difference in quality control between the two.

3. What does the workmanship warranty actually cover — and for how long? Read it. Most Colorado remodelers offer one year on labor. Ours is five.

4. Are permits pulled? Any project that moves plumbing or electrical past a fixture swap needs one in Littleton. A contractor who skips this is putting your homeowner's insurance and your resale disclosures at risk.

5. What's the waterproofing plan? If you get a blank stare, walk away. The correct answers involve a Schluter Kerdi membrane, RedGard, or a pre-manufactured sloped pan system — not tar paper and hope.

6. What happens if the project runs long? A serious contractor will commit to a fixed calendar, not "we should be done by whenever."

7. Can I buy the unit and install it myself? Ask, because the answer tells you a lot. Ours is yes — supply-only is available, and the manufacturer warranty stays in full effect as long as it's installed to manufacturer guidelines.

Permits & Code · Littleton, CO

What Colorado requires — and why it matters at resale.

Any bathroom remodel in Littleton that relocates plumbing, adds a circuit, moves a shower drain, or changes venting requires a permit from the City of Littleton (Arapahoe County), Jefferson County, or Douglas County depending on your parcel. Permits mean an inspector signs off that the work is code-compliant — which is exactly what a future buyer's inspection will scrutinize. Unpermitted bathroom work is one of the top items that kills or renegotiates real estate deals in the Denver metro. We pull permits as a matter of course. It's the boring answer, and it's the right one.

Aging in place · Universal design

Designing a bathroom that lets you stay for the next 20 years.

The CDC lists falls as the leading cause of injury for adults 65 and older, and the bathroom is where a large share of household falls happen — wet feet, hard surfaces, narrow doorways. A properly designed universal-design bathroom removes the specific hazards: curbless walk-in shower entry, a built-in bench, thermostatic anti-scald valves, comfort-height toilets, and grab bars anchored to solid blocking (not drywall anchors dressed up with a good story). Done right, none of it looks like a hospital. It just looks like a beautiful modern bathroom that happens to be safer.

Timing & Water Efficiency

When to remodel and what to pick to keep the water bill down.

The best time to remodel a bathroom in Littleton is late winter through early spring: crews are typically more available, materials aren't backordered, and if you're selling in the summer market you're finished with weeks to spare. Water efficiency also has real money attached to it. EPA WaterSense-labeled 1.28 gpf toilets, 1.5 gpm showerheads, and 1.2 gpm bathroom faucets typically save a Denver-metro family several thousand gallons a year without any noticeable performance drop — and Denver Water has periodic rebates for some of these fixtures. We include WaterSense-labeled options in every quote as the default; you can always upgrade.

Local knowledge

The four bathroom types we see most in Littleton — and what changes about the remodel.

Every neighborhood in Littleton has its own housing stock, and every housing stock has its own quirks. Here's what actually happens when we walk into each one for a bathroom consultation.

01 · The downtown historic

Bungalows and cottages, 1890s–1940s

Small footprints (often under 40 square feet), original hex tile or subway you may want to preserve, plaster walls, cast-iron drains, and framing that measures whatever it measured a hundred years ago. These bathrooms reward custom tile and vintage-appropriate fixtures over Crestline. We work around what's there, keep what's worth keeping, and quietly upgrade what isn't — modern GFCI, proper venting, and hidden waterproofing that the eye doesn't see but the wall behind the tile depends on.

02 · The suburban ranch

Split-levels and ranches, 1960s–1980s

The classic Columbine and Ken Caryl footprint: a hall bath with a builder-grade tub-shower combo, an original vanity, and vinyl flooring that has outlived its welcome by fifteen years. These are the sweet-spot projects for a Crestline turnkey install or a tub-to-shower conversion — big impact, moderate budget, one to four days on site. The plumbing rough-in is usually predictable and permits move quickly.

03 · The new-build master

Highlands Ranch, Roxborough, 2000s–2020s

Generous square footage, garden tubs that nobody uses, dated speckled granite, and standard-issue everything from the original builder. These are the projects where a full tile remodel really pays off — freestanding soaking tub or a big walk-in shower with a bench, custom vanity, waterproof LVT or heated tile floor, better lighting. Budget usually lands in the $15K–$25K range and the finished room is genuinely spa-caliber.

04 · The aging-in-place remodel

Any neighborhood, any age, planning ahead

Sometimes triggered by a specific event — a fall, a hip replacement, a spouse coming home from rehab — and sometimes by simple foresight. The design is universal: curbless walk-in shower or ADA-compliant walk-in tub, a fold-down bench, grab bars in the correct three spots anchored to real blocking, a comfort-height toilet, thermostatic anti-scald valve, non-slip flooring, and lighting bright enough to see clearly without glare. It's what Aging Safely Baths was founded on. We do a lot of these across every Littleton neighborhood.
